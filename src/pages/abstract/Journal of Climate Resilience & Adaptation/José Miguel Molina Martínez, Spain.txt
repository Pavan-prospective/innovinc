Southeast Spain 
 
Name: José Miguel Molina-Martínez 
Technical University of Cartagena, Spain 
Abstract: 
To respond to the critical challenges facing modern agriculture in the Southeast of Spain—such as 
climate change, recurring droughts, torrential rain, and excessive solar radiation—we have 
developed a resilient and sustainable agrivoltaic system. This innovative solution is designed to 
optimize solar radiation for crop development in real -time while providing a physical shield against 
extreme weather events. A core objective of this technology is to significantly reduce and eliminate 
the carbon footprint associated with intensive agricultural activities. By integrating renewable 
energy production directly into the farming infrastructure, the system powers its own automated 
irrigation and lighting technologies, replacing fossil fuel dependency with clean, on-site energy. 
The system features a mobile roof composed of solar panels that move in a coplanar manner to 
manage light transmission. During periods of peak radiation, the panels close to protect crops from 
thermal stress; during torrential rains, they close to act as a catchment surface. This allows for the 
intelligent collection and storage of rainwater for efficient irrigation, preventing the leaching of 
pollutants into aquifers and reducing surface runoff that contaminates nearby ecosystems. 
Furthermore, the system is equipped with luminescent spotlights powered by the stored 
photovoltaic energy to ensure optimal light spectra for plant growth even when the panels are 
closed. This integrated approach not only enhances climate resilience but also provides a 
transformative model for carbon-neutral, environmentally respectful horticulture. 
 
Biography  
José Miguel Molina -Martínez is a Professor and the current Director of the School of Agricultural 
Engineering at the Technical University of Cartagena (UPCT). Holding a PhD in Agricultural 
Engineering, he serves as the Principal Investigator of the "Agromotics and Sea Engineering" 
(IAM) R&D group. His research focuses on automation, remote control, and the efficient 
management of water and energy resources, with more than 40 articles published in high- impact 
journals (JCR Q1) and several patents currently in use. He has led numerous national and 
international projects centered on technological sustainability within the agri-food sector.
