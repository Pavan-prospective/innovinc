 in Arid Environments 
 
(*)Samia Haddadi1,2, Christine Cagnon1, Djamel Zeriri2, Cristiana Cravo-Laureau1, Robert Duran3 
1Universite de Pau et des Pays de l'Adour, E2S UPPA, CNRS, IPREM, Pau, France 2Sonatrach 
Division Laboratories, Avenue du 1er Novembre, Boumerdés, Algerie 
 
Abstract: 
Bioremediation is a promising, environmentally friendly approach for treating oil drilling waste, a 
challenge that is particularly acute in arid regions with intensive petroleum production. Effective 
bioaugmentation strategies require microbial consortia that are naturally adapted to such harsh 
environmental conditions. In this study, four metal(loid)-resistant, hydrocarbon-degrading microbial 
consortia capable of growing at 40 °C were isolated from oil drilling waste subjected to different 
phytomanagement conditions. These consortia displayed distinct microbial compositions and were 
able to degrade between 15% and 35% of total petroleum hydrocarbons within 15 days. The 
degradation processes produced varying hydrocarbon fraction profiles, supported by the 
identification of 14 specific OTUs determined through linear discriminant analysis effect size 
(LEfSe). Each consortium was characterized by groups of genera correlated with particular 
hydrocarbon fractions, accounting for their differences in degradation efficiency and resulting 
fraction profiles. Consequently, these consortia could be applied individually or sequentially to 
develop effective bioremediation strategies for managing multi -contaminated oil drilling waste in 
arid environments. 
 
Biography  
Samia Haddadi is a researcher with a PhD from the University of Pau and Pays de l’Adour, working 
in an Algerian oil company. Her work focuses on microbial diversity in arid ecosystems impacted 
by petroleum pollution. She characterizes microbial communities associated with endemic desert 
plants and other species thriving under extreme aridity. These stress -adapted microbial assemblages 
contribute to contaminant transformation, ecological stability, and resilience. By identifying 
climate-resistant microbial diversity, she develops bioprocesses that restore degraded arid 
environments under growing climatic and industrial pressures..
